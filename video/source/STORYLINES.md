# SAARTHI — Stage 2 pitch video: three storylines

Facts come only from the Stage 1 deck (SAARTHI_RAMAN_SamaypurShooters.pptx). Every one of the 9 Screen-2 points gets its own labelled chapter, using the deck's pill tag ("DRAWINGS · 3/9"). No prototype exists yet: point 2 is shown as a labelled "digital-twin simulation preview, illustrative", never as real footage.

Shared assumptions: 4:15–4:45 · 1920×1080, 30 fps · no voice, music bed only · deck look (white ground, lavender/sky/mint/peach/butter/rose pastels, ink #2D2A4A, Calibri, rounded cards, icons in pastel circles, DTU / SEVA / Delhi Govt logos).

---

## A — "One lap of the arena" (recommended)

**Device:** the object is the explanation. **Form:** a flight through one world.

A top-down pastel map of the DTU multi-environment track. The flat purple rover from the title slide drives one full lap: start line → lanes → signs and lights → potholes → 20° ramp → face-ID zone → laser → finish. At each stop the camera pushes in and opens the matching chapter. The ramp opens the traction calculation, the signs open the ML stack, and the lap itself is the simulation preview. After the finish line the camera pulls back to the "pit": an exploded rover for the BOM, three team bays, a 75-day road for the timeline, and hazard cones for gaps and risks.

- **First 2 s:** the rover sits on the start line of an empty pastel track. A 5 kg payload drops onto it, and the title "SAARTHI · one robot, every task, no human" types in.
- **Beats:** hook (rover + payload) → problem (each obstacle on the track is a task humans do today) → solution (three pillars bolt onto the rover) → simulation-preview lap → drawings (side/top view at the ramp) → calculations (ramp, stopping distance, 148→44 ms latency race) → BOM (exploded rover, prices pop on parts, ₹2.88 L total) → team (three owners light up their subsystems) → 75-day road with gates G0–G4 → gaps → risks and mitigations → end card with team + mentor.
- **Look:** the deck exactly. White ground, pastel fills, ink outlines, rounded 14 px cards, icon dots. The track is a soft lavender asphalt band with butter lane lines.
- **Sound:** warm, curious electronic pulse at ~100 bpm. Near-silent under the problem beat, opening up when SAARTHI is named. A riser into the finish line, which is the payoff. Soft pops on prices and gates.

## B — "The deck comes alive"

**Device:** type carries the argument. **Form:** one frame that rebuilds.

One pastel slide frame never cuts. Cards slide, merge and re-tile from chapter 1 to chapter 9, mirroring the jury checklist in order. It is closest to the PPT the judges have already seen, the easiest to verify against the rubric, and the safest. It is also the least memorable.

- **First 2 s:** the title slide, with the rover illustration rolling in from the right.
- **Beats:** title → 9 numbered chapters in the checklist order → end card.
- **Look:** the deck 1:1, including the section pills.
- **Sound:** light corporate-pastel bed at ~108 bpm, with a layout change on every bar.

## C — "148 milliseconds" (the bold one)

**Device:** a number lands. **Form:** split screen to one.

Opens on a split screen. A typical hobby robot (left) and SAARTHI (right) both approach the same pothole at 10 km/h. The left one reacts 42 cm late and clips it; the right one reacts after 12 cm and steers clear. That 148 ms → 44 ms number is the spine. The film then explains *why* SAARTHI is fast, moving outward layer by layer: the drone-grade core, the ML stack, the drawings, the calculations. Then *what it costs and who builds it*: BOM, team, 75 days, gaps, risks. It is the strongest hook for "technical feasibility", but it leans hard on one estimated number.

- **First 2 s:** two rovers, one pothole, a millisecond counter racing.
- **Beats:** latency race → why (three pillars) → layers 1–9 → the same race replayed at the end with "the 75-day plan to prove it".
- **Look:** the deck pastels with a single rose accent reserved for the latency number.
- **Sound:** tense minimal tick into a bright resolve when SAARTHI clears the pothole. Ticks on the counter.
